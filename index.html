Abstract

Psychiatric conditions are highly heritable and characterized by substantial genetic and phenotypic heterogeneity, yet robust electrophysiological biomarkers remain lacking. Whether this heterogeneity converges on shared neurophysiological alterations or instead reflects distinct disorder- and variant-specific signatures remains unclear. Here, we assembled the largest transdiagnostic resting-state EEG dataset to date, comprising 4,812 high-density recordings after quality control across 11 psychiatric conditions and multiple rare genetic variants spanning ages 5 months to 66 years. Using normative source-space modelling, we characterized developmental trajectories in a typically developing reference population (n = 1,082) and quantified individual-level deviations across clinical and genetic groups.

Across psychiatric conditions, deviations were small and strongly correlated across conditions, revealing a shared transdiagnostic profile characterized by disrupted spectral organization, connectivity, and signal complexity. In contrast, individual rare genetic variants showed substantially larger effects and non-convergent neurophysiological signatures. Notably, 16p11.2 deletions and duplications exhibited mirror-opposite deviation patterns across multiple EEG features. When rare variants were pooled, their aggregate profile converged toward the psychiatric conditions pattern despite strong heterogeneity at the level of individual variants. Across both clinical and genetic groups, deviations showed a consistent spatial organization concentrated within sensorimotor cortices and aligned with the principal gradient of cortical gene expression. Subsampling analyses further revealed that stable effect size estimates required substantially larger cohorts than typically used in the field: around 500 participants (250 cases and 250 controls) for psychiatric conditions, but as few as 60 (30 cases and 30 controls) for genetic variants.

Together, these findings establish a large-scale transdiagnostic normative framework for source-space EEG in psychiatric and genetic conditions and demonstrate that electrophysiological heterogeneity depends strongly on the level of biological stratification. These results provide empirical benchmarks for reproducibility and highlight how aggregation across genetically diverse etiologies can obscure variant-specific neurophysiological signatures.
